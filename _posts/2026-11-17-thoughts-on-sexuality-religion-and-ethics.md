I was born an Indian, assigned the gender male, assigned an unfairly privileged caste (Brahmin), and assigned a religion (Hinduism) that has, in modern days, curdled into instilling several morally grey and sometimes even outright dangerous values since childhood.

Now I've just turned 22, and several things have changed, even though the backdrop remains pretty much the same. I despise the atrocities committed by my caste’s ancestors and those still committed today. I also briefly got disillusioned about religion, God, and belief in a higher power, followed atheism internally, and eventually rediscovered the delights of having strong ethical beliefs and a feeling of community through Taoism. That is not to say that I now truly believe in the superstitions that come with any religion.

What I can say for sure is that I have been able to read into the subtext of the curdled values that had been inculcated in me and clear out wrong thoughts, bigoted mindset, and unethical reasoning. In summary of what I basically did, see the golden words of whoever coined the following Thirukkural (yes, I am from Tamil Nadu. I know people refer to the entity that wrote the text as Thiruvalluvar. But we do not know anything about this entity definitively):

> [சென்ற இடத்தால் செலவிடா தீதொரீஇ<br>நன்றின்பால் உய்ப்ப தறிவு](https://www.thirukkural.net/ta/kural/kural-0422.html)

Thus, I would like to call myself *religion-fluid*: I believe in the good parts (according to me) of both Hinduism and Taoism now. I still identify as a straight male, though the ways I would soon be describing that term might bring bitter disagreement to some readers and, to some, might be an enlightenment. I would prefer that, through this article, I can bring to you the latter experience.

To all the curious individuals about the last statements, I wouldn’t like to keep you waiting; hence, that is the first thing I will address, as it is also the first thing I think every human, especially men, should ponder at the very least.

## *A Logical View of Sex*

Take this age-old tale of [the blind men and an elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blind_men_and_an_elephant). In short, it is about a group of blind people who have never encountered an elephant… nah, let me make this article more engaging.

I will now talk about a past life in a village, when I was blind, with superpowers like [Daredevil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daredevil_(TV_series)): call me Sathya. I had a few friends who were also blind, with superpowers like Daredevil: Kushant, Noor, Bhagat, Janaki, and Salim. It was very, very long ago, and the society was very rigid and close-minded. In the middle of the night, we heard moans from a great distance that we could locate, thanks to our Daredevil superpowers. Moans of a man and a woman. Initially, their moans were filled with pleasure, then some whispers followed. The woman said, “Please stop,” and the man said, “Just a bit more,” and continued. We heard some sc<strong><em>rape</em></strong>y sounds over what we surmised was a skin-like surface. The moans were now filled: one with pleasure and one with dread. We all rushed, atop the buildings, through the alleys, over the dirt roads, downstairs, upstairs, and forward, finally breaking into the source’s door. We were all equal, so we all reached there at the same time, even though our paths’ lengths weren’t equal. The coupling stopped, and the man’s voice turned around, faced us, and said, “You mad, blind people. How dare you barge into a married couple’s room at night?” Anyway, we were then escorted to the village’s common well. A *panchayat* was convened, and all six of us were labelled *heretics*. I think they didn't like our superpowers like Daredevil.

We were held in contempt for interrupting the private life of a married couple. All six of us said, “We barged in because we didn’t think that was sex.” The *panchayat* committee, all six of them - not blind and without any superpowers like Daredevil - laughed at us and asked, “Then what was it?”

“We don’t know. Ask both the married individuals, separately, promising neither of them any unjust repercussions for telling the truth. If they both agree that everything they experienced throughout was something they freely chose, then it was sex, and we are heretics. If either one doesn’t, then it was not sex. In the latter case, we stopped just what was not sex. We don’t know what it was, or to whom it was what. That would need proper investigation.”

*This is the logical view of sex.* Now, about the other word, sex. Let me continue the story for that.

The six of them laughed again and asked the man, who nodded yes. And they concluded we were heretics. We were about to be sentenced to death. But we had an out. The *panchayat* committee gave us a test. According to them, it apparently could completely acquit us of our heresy if we passed it. They will present some entity to us. We feel the entity at different points, try to guess what the entity is, and conclude without argument.

I now repeat, from what I remember, what I declared and what my friends declared as their sensations, when they felt the entity at different points. I, Sathya, felt the top; I felt some tapering cone. Kushant felt through. So the entity had some sort of gap between two solid structures, at least in a topological sense. Noor apparently grazed over a concave cup-like structure on one side. Bhagat felt some solid petals; Janaki felt a slightly flat surface in the middle and two wide, horizontally elliptical pebbles, eye-like at the ends of the flat surface; and Salim felt a snake-like solid structure with intricate, evenly spaced horizontal carvings. We are all equal, so we all reached the same right answer, even though our paths weren’t equal. It is a well-carved statue of an elephant holding a lotus in its lap, with a crown on its head.

We received the death sentence because we were wrong, and it was God.

I think they didn't like that we were blind. However, we perceived what we perceived. Even though all of that was just a thought experiment in our current world, I believe Sathya was then probably [reborn as me in this world](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h6fcK_fRYaI), as I am today. I still strive to perceive what I perceive as if I were the blind Sathya. And my friends equally should be allowed to perceive what they perceive as if they were the blind Sathya’s blind friends. And those who are not my friends should strive to be blind. I don’t mean that they should go blind and touch to perceive. They should go blind to the *panchayat* in their minds and *perceive and accept what my friends perceive*. Then they become my friends.

So: [*As a straight male, if I perceive someone as feminine, and I am attracted to them and their presence, personality, and existence, then if I am single, I would not hesitate to go out with them, given the opportunity, their enthusiastic consent, and their enthusiastic acceptance of my perception of them.*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friend_of_Dorothy)

*This is the logical view of my sex.* And that was it for my article; hope you got enlightened. You ask, *“WAIT. WHERE’S THE REST?”* For the rest, you can just read it again, two more times if needed. And that was it for my article; hope you got enlightened.
